Market Nerve · design brief for the design team

Everything you need to redesign this app, in one file.

Market Nerve is a behavioural-finance app for iOS and Android: you replay real historical market crashes with the asset hidden, and it grades your nerve rather than your luck. This document is what the app does, how it works, a wireframe of every screen — including the three that do not exist yet — and the design system exactly as it stands in code.

Sections 01–13 explain the product and show the screens. Sections 14–22 are the working reference: tokens, components, and the rules a redesign has to respect. Wireframes show structure and intent only — proportions are indicative and nothing in them is a visual proposal.

01 What it is

Market Nerve is a behavioural-finance simulator that grades your nerve, not your stock-picking. You are dropped into a real historical market crash with the asset and the dates hidden. The chart replays at speed, your portfolio value falls in front of you, and at specific moments playback stops and asks one question: hold, sell everything, or buy more? Afterwards it tells you what actually happened next, scores the discipline of your choices, and reveals which crash you just lived through.

The problem it addresses

Everyone knows the advice — don't panic-sell in a downturn. Almost nobody can do it, because reading the advice and watching your own money fall are unrelated experiences. This app manufactures the second experience safely, with ₹1,00,000 of virtual capital, so the lesson is felt rather than recited.

Who it is for

  • New retail investors in India, primarily — people who have started investing in the last few years and have not yet been through a serious drawdown. The campaign is weighted towards Indian market events for exactly this reason.
  • Anyone who sold at the bottom once and would rather understand why than repeat it.
  • Not for traders looking for a backtester. There is no strategy engine, no orders, no real money, anywhere.

02 Non-negotiables

Five rules constrain the visual design. They come from app-store review and from the product's premise, not from taste — a redesign that breaks one of them cannot ship, so please treat them as fixed and push back on us if they get in the way.

01
Anything simulated must say so, permanently. Every screen holding virtual money carries a visible SIMULATED · ₹1,00,000 virtual capital badge, during play and at the debrief. It cannot be a tooltip, a one-time toast, or hidden behind a scroll. It has to survive a screenshot, because reviewers look at screenshots.
02
Live Markets carries the opposite label. It is the one screen showing real prices, so it must say real, possibly delayed, and no orders placeable — and must not wear the SIMULATED badge. Blurring the two is what would make the badge meaningless everywhere else.
03
Blind mode may not leak the asset. During a run, no real price, ticker or date may appear anywhere on screen — axes and readouts included. Prices are rebased so the first bar reads 100 and days are counted D14. If a new design surfaces a number, check with us where it comes from. The reveal happens only at the debrief, and it is the payoff.
04
Every live number names its source. Real prices display their provider and whether they are delayed — Yahoo Finance · delayed, Binance · live. It is small type, but it is not optional and it needs a home in any new layout.
05
A control we cannot serve is absent, not disabled. We removed an entire layer of deliberately-dead "coming soon" chrome. Nothing in the UI should now look tappable and do nothing. If a screen cannot offer an option, it does not draw it — a campaign level has daily data only, so its chart offers 1D/1W/1M and no intraday at all.

03 The mechanic

One engine runs every mode. A "level" is just data — a price series plus a list of moments to stop at — so a curated 2008 crash and a run on a stock the user picked this morning are the same thing to the app.

Where pause points come from

Not hand-picked. The app walks the price series tracking a running peak and asks a question each time price falls a further step below wherever it last asked, provided it is also meaningfully below that peak. A new high resets the ladder, so a second leg down starts asking again. This matters to you for one reason: a level has 4–14 pause points, not a fixed number, so any design that counts them has to stretch.

The two ways to play

ModePlaybackInputBottom panel
Beginner Stops at each pause point and waits. Exactly three buttons. No timer. Decision panel, only while halted.
Advanced Never stops. Runs to the end. Buy or sell any fraction, any moment. Trade panel, always present.

Both are graded on the same behavioural question — did you cut your position while it was falling? — so scores stay comparable. The user picks the mode on the campaign home before starting, not inside the run.

04 How a call is scored

The Discipline Score is the app's headline number and appears in three places, so it is worth understanding what it rewards.

A design consequence. Some moments are genuinely ambiguous — the data does not support a clean right answer — and the debrief says so in words. A design that renders every call as simply right or wrong will contradict the copy. Three outcomes need visual form, not two.

05 Navigation map

Four tabs, each with its own navigator so a run in progress survives a trip to another tab. Everything below a tab is pushed on top of it.

07 Level screen — the core

The screen that matters. Five stacked regions compete for height, and its four states differ only in what occupies the bottom third — which is exactly what makes the pause moment land.

Region budget, top to bottom

  1. 1App bar, ~22 pt. Close button and the run mode. The only exit.
  2. 2Header, ~44 pt. Masked ticker, day counter, portfolio value, P&L pill, progress rail, and the virtual-capital line. Six things; the most crowded region in the app.
  3. 3Chart, flexible. Takes whatever is left. Every indicator pane the user opens steals height from it.
  4. 4Chart toolbar, 76 pt. Hidden until the run starts, because the brief needs the room.
  5. 5Transport, ~20 pt. Play/pause and three real speeds — 1×, 2×, 4×.
  6. 6Bottom panel, 30–114 pt. Decision panel, trade panel, or the run-complete bar. Takes space from the chart rather than overlaying it, on purpose: overlaying hid the low the player was reacting to.
The hardest constraint in the app. On a 375 × 667 screen those six regions do not comfortably fit, and we have already patched it twice — hiding the toolbar pre-run, and pinning the START RUN button because the brief was clipping it. A designed answer to "which region yields height first" would be worth more than anything else on this page.

08 Debrief — the payoff

A single scrolling screen. It is the only place the blind is lifted, and it has to do three jobs at once: score you, tell you what the crash was, and explain each call without lecturing.

The acquisition loop. No account, no gameplay, three fields and a shareable card. It is the only screen whose output is designed to leave the app.

12 Daily Pivot — to be designed

No screens exist. This is a proposal to argue with, not a spec to follow — the flow and the constraints are fixed, the design is entirely yours.

Fixed rules for this pillar

  • Points, never money, stated near the vote buttons. Non-negotiable.
  • One vote per user per day, and it cannot be changed after locking.
  • The crowd percentage is a real aggregate. Never a placeholder that looks finished.
  • Being right and contrarian pays more than being right with the crowd — the scoring rewards independent thinking, which is the same thesis as the Simulator.
  • A 9am local notification opens this tab. Design the state it lands on.

15 Type

Two bundled variable faces, no runtime fetching, so Android and iOS render numbers identically. The split is strict and it is the single highest-leverage rule in the system: every number is monospace, all prose is Inter.

RoleFaceSizeWeightTrackingUsed for
titleInter22 600−0.4 Screen headings.
bodyInter14 4000 / lh 1.45 Narrative copy, reveals, explanations.
monoJetBrains Mono14 5000 Prices, scores, ₹ amounts, timestamps. Tabular figures always on, so digits do not jitter as the replay ticks.
labelJetBrains Mono10 700+1.1 Uppercase console chrome — DAY 14/130, SIMULATED, section headers.

Large numerals set in JetBrains Mono are the app's signature. Where a figure is the point of a screen, it is set big, monospace and tight.

Motion

One easing curve across the whole app — cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), expo-out: fast departure, soft landing. Three durations only: 140 ms for state flips, 240 ms for standard transitions, 380 ms for deliberate reveals. Inconsistent timing is the fastest way to make an interface feel cheap, so new motion should reuse these rather than introduce a fourth.

One exception, and it is deliberate: the chart has no entry animation. At 4× a tween per candle turns fast-forward into mush. The replay's motion comes from new candles arriving, not from lerping.

Constraints a redesign has to respect

  • The toolbar is currently two rows totalling 76 pt, and it is hidden until a run starts because the pre-run brief needs the height. On a 375 × 667 screen the vertical budget is genuinely tight — every pane the user opens takes height from the candles.
  • In blind mode the axes must render rebased index values and day counters. The chart never formats a value itself; it asks a labels object, which is what stops a new feature leaking the asset.
  • Volume is unavailable on most campaign levels — the data has none. The legend says so rather than drawing an empty pane, and any redesign needs an answer for that state.

20 Open questions for you

Things we decided by engineering judgement and would rather a designer settled. None of these block you starting.

Level screen Five stacked regions on a 375 × 667 screen. Which one loses height first? Header, chart, toolbar, transport bar and the decision panel all compete. We already had to hide the toolbar before a run starts and pin the START RUN button because the brief was clipping it. A designed answer would be better than our patches.
Level screen Should the decision panel overlay the chart or take space from it? It currently takes space. Overlaying looked tidier but hid the bottom of the drawdown — the player could not see the low they were being asked to react to. If you can solve both, that is a real win.
Debrief The Discipline Score is a big number called a dial. Should it be one? It is the app's headline metric and appears in three places. A real gauge might earn its space — or might be decoration on a number that already reads.
Campaign Is ████ the right mask for an unplayed level? The tile must not name the event, but it should still feel enticing rather than redacted. Right now it reads as censorship.
System Empty, loading and error states are inconsistent. They were written per screen as each was built. A single pattern for all three, applied across the app, would tidy a lot at once.

21 What to draw first

If you want the fastest route to something we can ship, in this order.

  1. 1The Daily Pivot's four states. Nothing exists, nothing to undo, and it is a whole missing pillar. State 3 — the crowd reveal — is the single highest-value screen in the app right now.
  2. 2The level screen's height problem. Six regions, 667 pt. Tell us which one yields and we will build it. This unblocks the screen everything else serves.
  3. 3The paywall. Blocks release outright, and it is one screen.
  4. 4One empty / loading / error pattern to replace the five inconsistent ones.
  5. 5App icon and the two store screenshots. Needed at submission, and the hero shot is a pause point mid-crash.
Two moving parts to know about. The campaign has 21 levels specified and 17 with sourced data, and the data licensing is unresolved — so the level count may drop and the map should survive a smaller campaign. And the Kotak Neo broker connection may never ship; nothing should depend on it existing.

If anything on this page disagrees with the running app, the app is right — tell us and we will fix the page.

22 Handing work back

What makes a design fastest for us to implement.

  • Name tokens, not values. "surfaceRaised" rather than "#1B222B" tells us you mean the existing token; a raw hex reads as a request for a new one. New tokens are fine — just say so explicitly.
  • Design at 390 × 844, check at 375 × 667. The short phone is where our layouts have broken, three times so far.
  • Mark every number as mono. If a figure in your design is set in Inter, we will assume it is a mistake and change it, because the rule is absolute in code.
  • Flag anything that touches the five rules in section 01 so we catch it in review rather than at store submission.
  • Give us the states. Empty, loading, error and the long-content case. We will build whichever ones you draw and improvise the rest.

Token definitions live in lib/app/theme.dart; the written design rules in DESIGN.md; product constraints in CLAUDE.md. If a rule here and the code ever disagree, the code is what ships — tell us and we will fix the document.
