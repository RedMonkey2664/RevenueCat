Design handoff · Market Nerve

Everything a designer needs to redraw this app.

Market Nerve is a behavioural-finance app for iOS and Android: you replay real historical crashes with the asset hidden, and it grades your nerve rather than your luck. This page is the working state of the interface — the tokens as they exist in code, every screen and whether it is finished, and the constraints that are not ours to move.

01 Non-negotiables

Five rules constrain the visual design. They come from app-store review and from the product's premise, not from taste — a redesign that breaks one of them cannot ship, so please treat them as fixed and push back on us if they get in the way.

01
Anything simulated must say so, permanently. Every screen holding virtual money carries a visible SIMULATED · ₹1,00,000 virtual capital badge, during play and at the debrief. It cannot be a tooltip, a one-time toast, or hidden behind a scroll. It has to survive a screenshot, because reviewers look at screenshots.
02
Live Markets carries the opposite label. It is the one screen showing real prices, so it must say real, possibly delayed, and no orders placeable — and must not wear the SIMULATED badge. Blurring the two is what would make the badge meaningless everywhere else.
03
Blind mode may not leak the asset. During a run, no real price, ticker or date may appear anywhere on screen — axes and readouts included. Prices are rebased so the first bar reads 100 and days are counted D14. If a new design surfaces a number, check with us where it comes from. The reveal happens only at the debrief, and it is the payoff.
04
Every live number names its source. Real prices display their provider and whether they are delayed — Yahoo Finance · delayed, Binance · live. It is small type, but it is not optional and it needs a home in any new layout.
05
A control we cannot serve is absent, not disabled. We removed an entire layer of deliberately-dead "coming soon" chrome. Nothing in the UI should now look tappable and do nothing. If a screen cannot offer an option, it does not draw it — a campaign level has daily data only, so its chart offers 1D/1W/1M and no intraday at all.

02 The product

Four tabs. The Simulator is the reason the app exists; the other three exist to bring people back to it or to bring people in.

Simulator Core

A real crash replays at speed with the asset hidden. At scripted moments it stops and asks one question — Hold, Sell All, or Buy the Dip — then grades you against what the data actually did next. 21 campaign levels, an endless mode, and any instrument you choose.

Live Markets Context

A watchlist of real current prices across Indian equity, US equity and crypto, each opening a full chart. Read-only. It is also the way into a custom simulation.

Daily Pivot Retention

One yes/no call on Bitcoin each morning, then the crowd's split is revealed and Discipline Points are awarded. Points, never money. Not built — needs design.

Time Machine Growth

"What would ₹80,000 in 2018 be now?" — with a shareable result card. No account, no gameplay, works in seconds. It is the funnel.

The moment everything serves

The chart is racing, the portfolio number is falling, the screen flashes red, playback stops, and three buttons appear. That pause is the product. Most of the polish budget belongs on the level screen and the debrief that follows it — if a change makes that moment land harder, it is worth disrupting the rest of the app for.

04 What to design while we build

These three can be designed in parallel with our engineering, because we have not written the screens yet — there is nothing for a design to contradict. In rough order of value to us.

1 · Daily Pivot — the whole pillar

One question a day, answered in under ten seconds, that makes someone open the app tomorrow. The flow has four states, and the third is the one that matters:

  • Ask — today's yes/no question about Bitcoin, and two buttons.
  • Voted — locked in, waiting. This state may last hours.
  • Crowd reveal — the split, and whether you went with or against it. This is the emotional beat and it currently has no design at all.
  • Resolved — what actually happened, and Discipline Points awarded, with a bonus for being correct and contrarian.

Two hard constraints: points are never money and the screen must say so near the buttons; and the crowd percentage must be a real aggregate, so please do not design a state that implies precision we cannot deliver on low vote counts. A streak indicator lives here too.

2 · Paywall

Driven by RevenueCat. The free/paid line is already decided: the first level of each market is free, the rest of the campaign is paid, and endless mode is paid. Everything else — Daily Pivot, Time Machine, Live Markets — stays free. It needs the offer, a restore-purchases affordance, and to not feel like a wall in the middle of the best moment in the app.

3 · App icon and store screenshots

The hero screenshot should be a pause point mid-crash — the flash, the falling number, the three buttons — because that is the product in one frame. A Time Machine share card is the natural second. The icon has no existing direction; the in-app mark is a pair of candlesticks.

Worth knowing before you start. The campaign has 21 levels specified and 17 with real sourced data. The data licensing for those levels is still unresolved, so level count may drop. Design the level map to survive a smaller campaign — do not build a layout that only reads well at exactly 21 tiles.

06 Type

Two bundled variable faces, no runtime fetching, so Android and iOS render numbers identically. The split is strict and it is the single highest-leverage rule in the system: every number is monospace, all prose is Inter.

titleInter22 600−0.4 Screen headings.
bodyInter14 4000 / lh 1.45 Narrative copy, reveals, explanations.
monoJetBrains Mono14 5000 Prices, scores, ₹ amounts, timestamps. Tabular figures always on, so digits do not jitter as the replay ticks.
labelJetBrains Mono10 700+1.1 Uppercase console chrome — DAY 14/130, SIMULATED, section headers.

Specimen

Large numerals set in JetBrains Mono are the app's signature. Where a figure is the point of a screen, it is set big, monospace and tight.

Motion

One easing curve across the whole app — cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), expo-out: fast departure, soft landing. Three durations only: 140 ms for state flips, 240 ms for standard transitions, 380 ms for deliberate reveals. Inconsistent timing is the fastest way to make an interface feel cheap, so new motion should reuse these rather than introduce a fourth.

One exception, and it is deliberate: the chart has no entry animation. At 4× a tween per candle turns fast-forward into mush. The replay's motion comes from new candles arriving, not from lerping.

09 The chart

One custom-drawn chart serves the Simulator, Live Markets and custom simulations. It is the most complex surface in the app and the one most likely to constrain a redesign, so here is exactly what it can do.

InteractionDrag to pan, pinch to zoom about the focal bar, long-press for a crosshair with an OHLC readout, drag the price gutter to scale it, double-tap to reset.

Constraints a redesign has to respect

  • The toolbar is currently two rows totalling 76 pt, and it is hidden until a run starts because the pre-run brief needs the height. On a 375 × 667 screen the vertical budget is genuinely tight — every pane the user opens takes height from the candles.
  • In blind mode the axes must render rebased index values and day counters. The chart never formats a value itself; it asks a labels object, which is what stops a new feature leaking the asset.
  • Volume is unavailable on most campaign levels — the data has none. The legend says so rather than drawing an empty pane, and any redesign needs an answer for that state.

10 Open questions for you

Things we decided by engineering judgement and would rather a designer settled. None of these block you starting.

Level screen Five stacked regions on a 375 × 667 screen. Which one loses height first? Header, chart, toolbar, transport bar and the decision panel all compete. We already had to hide the toolbar before a run starts and pin the START RUN button because the brief was clipping it. A designed answer would be better than our patches.
Level screen Should the decision panel overlay the chart or take space from it? It currently takes space. Overlaying looked tidier but hid the bottom of the drawdown — the player could not see the low they were being asked to react to. If you can solve both, that is a real win.
Debrief The Discipline Score is a big number called a dial. Should it be one? It is the app's headline metric and appears in three places. A real gauge might earn its space — or might be decoration on a number that already reads.
Campaign Is ████ the right mask for an unplayed level? The tile must not name the event, but it should still feel enticing rather than redacted. Right now it reads as censorship.
System Empty, loading and error states are inconsistent. They were written per screen as each was built. A single pattern for all three, applied across the app, would tidy a lot at once.

11 Handing work back

What makes a design fastest for us to implement.

  • Name tokens, not values. "surfaceRaised" rather than "#1B222B" tells us you mean the existing token; a raw hex reads as a request for a new one. New tokens are fine — just say so explicitly.
  • Design at 390 × 844, check at 375 × 667. The short phone is where our layouts have broken, three times so far.
  • Mark every number as mono. If a figure in your design is set in Inter, we will assume it is a mistake and change it, because the rule is absolute in code.
  • Flag anything that touches the five rules in section 01 so we catch it in review rather than at store submission.
  • Give us the states. Empty, loading, error and the long-content case. We will build whichever ones you draw and improvise the rest.

Token definitions live in lib/app/theme.dart; the written design rules in DESIGN.md; product constraints in CLAUDE.md. If a rule here and the code ever disagree, the code is what ships — tell us and we will fix the document.
